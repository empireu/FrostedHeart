# Calculated Sky From Nanobot And Ice Scattering

- Time: `2026-11-05 21:16:30 +0200`
- Authors: `empireu; Claude (Anthropic, claude-opus-5-5) as implementing agent`
- Status: `in-progress`
- Scope: overworld sky, fog colour, and daylight colour on the client; `content/climate/render/sky`
- Related: `design/lore.md` and `design/world-design.md` (nanobots of titanium dioxide, about `100 nm`), [current behaviour](../docs/climate/calculated-sky.md), [weather rendering](../docs/climate/weather-rendering.md)

## Goal

Calculate the sky from the lore's particles instead of fixed biome colours, when no shader pack is active. One model should eventually drive the sky, the fog colour, and the daylight colour.

## Verified Current State

- Vanilla `LevelRenderer.renderSky` draws a flat-coloured dome from `ClientLevel.getSkyColor`, then the sunrise fan from `DimensionSpecialEffects.getSunriseColor`, then sun, moon, and stars.
- `FogModification.renderFogColors` changes the fog colour only when `prevFogDensity > 0`, so clear-weather fog is vanilla.
- Oculus `1.20.1-1.7.0` and Embeddium `0.3.31` add only `@Inject` hooks to `renderSky` and `getSunriseColor`, so an inject after the dome and an override of `getSunriseColor` do not conflict with them.
- FrostedHeart does not use `IrisApi` elsewhere. Oculus is `compileOnly` and `clientRuntimeOnly`.

## Decisions

- Method: Hillaire 2020 lookup tables, plus a term for light reflected between snow and atmosphere. Its formula is in the docs. Single scattering in the style of Unity's The Blacksmith was compared offline and rejected as too dark and too olive over snow.
- Tables are built on the CPU, on `Util.backgroundExecutor()`. The Java model was checked against an offline Monte Carlo reference, and a GLSL port would need checking again. This also avoids float render targets.
- Vanilla draws the sun, moon, and stars, so only the dome is replaced. This keeps vanilla's exits, moon phases, and star fade without copying them.
- On `2026-11-07` empireu chose to replace vanilla's sun with one the shader draws, after vanilla's square core and glow showed inside the sky's own glow. It has the size and place of vanilla's core, and each texel takes the transmittance of its own elevation, rather than one color for the whole square, so the horizon cuts it off at sunset. empireu asked for it to glide smoothly. Edges on sky texels made the square seem to change shape as it moved, so its outline follows screen pixels while its color follows the sky texels. The moon and stars stay vanilla.
- The sky toggles at runtime by config and command. Testing compares on and off in one game instance at a frozen time of day.
- Default off until the maintainers decide.
- On `2026-11-07` empireu chose a halo display from hexagonal ice crystals over the three-part ice phase, with the old ring model dropped. Halos come from formulas over tables that the offline preview ray traced, shipped as raw data in `assets/frostedheart/datasets/ice_halos` after the dataset convention of ElectricalAge2. The generator stays in the preview for now. It may later move into the mod so it can run on demand.
- The whole sky is drawn on square texels fixed to the sky, like the sun's texture (`texelDegrees`, `0` for smooth). Texels fixed to the screen were rejected, because thin arcs would crawl as the camera turns. The default is `0.5 degrees`, which empireu chose after `1 degree` looked coarse in game. The halo map has `0.5 degree` bins, so finer texels show halos at that detail.
- Ice optical depth stays at `0.1` for now, to compare with `0.3` in game.

## Steps

1. Done in the working tree: sky dome, config, client command, GameTests, docs. After the first in-game run on `2026-11-05`, the ice phase function was split into a diffraction peak, a broad lobe, and the ring, because one lobe with asymmetry `0.8` filled the inside of the halo with glow. Snow now fades the sky to flat grey, because the halo stayed visible through snowfall.
2. Done in the working tree on `2026-11-06`: fog colour from the model's horizon colour outside snow, so terrain fades into the sky. Comparison screenshots showed a cold band under the warm sunset sky, because removing the sunrise fan also removed vanilla's orange fog tint, and a band in snow, because the sky faded to a grey that differed from the pack's snow fog. The sky in snow now fades toward the snow fog instead.
3. Done in the working tree on `2026-11-07`: ice crystal halos (22 and 46 degree halos, sun dogs, parhelic circle, tangent arcs, circumzenithal arc, Parry arcs) and the sky on texels fixed to the sky. A halo map is built on one background thread every `0.5 degrees` of sun elevation, and the two maps around the sun are blended by its exact elevation. Its sampling was made dense enough for `0.5 degree` texels, about `0.45 s` per map in the preview instead of `0.2 s`, after empireu saw the arcs break into colored dots at `0.5` and `0.25 degrees`. A compute shader was considered and set aside: macOS has no OpenGL 4.3, the CPU needs the map for the ground light, and the GameTests have no GL. Later that day the build was made about `2.65` times faster without changing the halos, mostly by dropping repeated trigonometry in the ray tracers: about `82 ms` per map for the mod's own code, measured outside the game on one thread, against `218 ms` before. Merging ray tracer outputs that leave in the same direction then brought a map to about `73 ms`, and working out the view rays once per setting brought a sky view from about `28` to `15.5 ms`, with the same results. Faster sine, cosine, and exponential approximations and 32 bit floats were measured and left out, at about `2 ms` and no gain per map. Then the shader drew its own sun and vanilla's was hidden while the calculated sky is drawn. With the day cycle running, the sky around a low sun changed visibly at every new sky view, so the two views around the sun are now blended like the halo maps.
4. Daylight colour through `IForgeDimensionSpecialEffects.adjustLightmapColors`. Tint first, brightness later if at all.
5. Later: depth-based aerial perspective, clouds, ice and density that follow weather, and lore events such as swarms of narrow particle sizes, which turn the sky teal, lilac, or yellow-green offline.

Open tuning questions: the low sun is dim compared with vanilla, a halo on every clear day may be too much, and the night has no moonlight.

## Validation

- `runGameTestServer -PgameTestNamespaces=frostedheart_calculated_sky` for the optics.
- In `runClient`, with the pack colour preview datapack, at a frozen time of day: screenshots and frame rate with the sky on and off, at sunrise, noon, sunset, and night, in clear weather and snow. Check sun and moon placement, GL state leaks (hand, HUD, clouds, weather, translucent blocks, infrared view, white curtain), resize, resource reload, graphics modes, other dimensions, fluids, and blindness.
- Target for the draw: under `1 ms` per frame at 1080p.

## Outcome

Open. On `2026-11-05` the first version worked in `runClient`: toggle, halo centred on the sun, no seam at the horizon, night like vanilla, and the frame rate at the `144` cap both ways. The ice and snow changes of `2026-11-06` pass the GameTests and were seen in `runClient` the same day: a thin ring with a red inner edge and a darker inside, and a faint ring in snow, which empireu chose to keep. The fog colour from step 2 passes the GameTests and was seen in game the same day: the sunset horizon is warm and meets the sky, and in snow the sky meets the snow fog without a band. Step 3 passes the GameTests on `2026-11-07` and was first seen in game the same day. The halo display shows as in the preview. empireu chose `0.5 degree` texels and found the denser sampling for them fine in game. The faster build, which also builds halo maps and sky views without allocating, checked out in game the same day, and so did the merged ray tracer outputs and the view rays worked out once per setting, which empireu found sufficient. The shader's own sun was seen in game the same day: striking with a low sun, dimmed by snow, and in its place at all five test times. With the day cycle running it seemed to change from one state to the other, which the blended views and pixel edges fixed, as empireu confirmed in game.
