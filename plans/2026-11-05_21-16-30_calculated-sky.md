# Calculated Sky From Nanobot And Ice Scattering

- Time: `2026-11-05 21:16:30 +0200`
- Authors: `empireu; Claude (Anthropic, claude-opus-5-5) as implementing agent`
- Status: `in-progress`
- Scope: overworld sky, fog colour, and daylight colour on the client; `content/climate/render/sky`
- Related: `design/lore.md` and `design/world-design.md` (nanobots of titanium dioxide, about `100 nm`), [current behaviour](../docs/climate/calculated-sky.md), [weather rendering](../docs/climate/weather-rendering.md)

## Goal

Calculate the sky from the lore's particles instead of fixed biome colours, when no shader pack is active. One model should eventually drive the sky, the fog colour, and the daylight colour.

## Verified Current State

- Vanilla `LevelRenderer.renderSky` draws a flat-coloured dome from `ClientLevel.getSkyColor`, then the sunrise fan from `DimensionSpecialEffects.getSunriseColor`, then sun, moon, and stars.
- `FogModification.renderFogColors` changes the fog colour only when `prevFogDensity > 0`, so clear-weather fog is vanilla.
- Oculus `1.20.1-1.7.0` and Embeddium `0.3.31` add only `@Inject` hooks to `renderSky` and `getSunriseColor`, so an inject after the dome and an override of `getSunriseColor` do not conflict with them.
- FrostedHeart does not use `IrisApi` elsewhere. Oculus is `compileOnly` and `clientRuntimeOnly`.

## Decisions

- Method: Hillaire 2020 lookup tables, plus a term for light reflected between snow and atmosphere. Its formula is in the docs. Single scattering in the style of Unity's The Blacksmith was compared offline and rejected as too dark and too olive over snow.
- Tables are built on the CPU, on `Util.backgroundExecutor()`. The Java model was checked against an offline Monte Carlo reference, and a GLSL port would need checking again. This also avoids float render targets.
- Vanilla draws the sun, moon, and stars, so only the dome is replaced. This keeps vanilla's exits, moon phases, and star fade without copying them.
- The sky toggles at runtime by config and command. Testing compares on and off in one game instance at a frozen time of day.
- Default off until the maintainers decide.

## Steps

1. Done in the working tree: sky dome, config, client command, GameTests, docs. After the first in-game run on `2026-11-05`, the ice phase function was split into a diffraction peak, a broad lobe, and the ring, because one lobe with asymmetry `0.8` filled the inside of the halo with glow. Snow now fades the sky to flat grey, because the halo stayed visible through snowfall.
2. Done in the working tree on `2026-11-06`: fog colour from the model's horizon colour outside snow, so terrain fades into the sky. Comparison screenshots showed a cold band under the warm sunset sky, because removing the sunrise fan also removed vanilla's orange fog tint, and a band in snow, because the sky faded to a grey that differed from the pack's snow fog. The sky in snow now fades toward the snow fog instead.
3. Daylight colour through `IForgeDimensionSpecialEffects.adjustLightmapColors`. Tint first, brightness later if at all.
4. Later: depth-based aerial perspective, clouds, ice and density that follow weather, and lore events such as swarms of narrow particle sizes, which turn the sky teal, lilac, or yellow-green offline.

Open tuning questions: the low sun is dim compared with vanilla, a halo on every clear day may be too much, and the night has no moonlight.

## Validation

- `runGameTestServer -PgameTestNamespaces=frostedheart_calculated_sky` for the optics.
- In `runClient`, with the pack colour preview datapack, at a frozen time of day: screenshots and frame rate with the sky on and off, at sunrise, noon, sunset, and night, in clear weather and snow. Check sun and moon placement, GL state leaks (hand, HUD, clouds, weather, translucent blocks, infrared view, white curtain), resize, resource reload, graphics modes, other dimensions, fluids, and blindness.
- Target for the draw: under `1 ms` per frame at 1080p.

## Outcome

Open. On `2026-11-05` the first version worked in `runClient`: toggle, halo centred on the sun, no seam at the horizon, night like vanilla, and the frame rate at the `144` cap both ways. The ice and snow changes of `2026-11-06` pass the GameTests and were seen in `runClient` the same day: a thin ring with a red inner edge and a darker inside, and a faint ring in snow, which empireu chose to keep. The fog colour from step 2 passes the GameTests and was seen in game the same day: the sunset horizon is warm and meets the sky, and in snow the sky meets the snow fog without a band.
