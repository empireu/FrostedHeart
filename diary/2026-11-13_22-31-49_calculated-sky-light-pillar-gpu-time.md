## Completed

Light pillars cost less GPU time, after `2026-11-13_19-46-03_calculated-sky-light-pillar-glitter.md` (`5d02380dc`).

- Ribbons hold glints only from `LightPillarOptics.GLINT_VALUE` on, eight display steps, instead of from `VISIBLE_VALUE`, half a step, with `RIBBON_GLINT_CHANCE` at `1%` instead of `0.2%`, and hold the smooth pillar down to `VISIBLE_VALUE` as before. `ribbonHalfWidth` with a count takes both values and returns the wider of the two widths, and `halfWidthAt` in `light_pillars.glsl` does the same with the new uniform `glintValue`. `leastVisibleCount` counts a crystal as reaching the glint value from `10%` below it on (`RIBBON_GLINT_MARGIN`).
- The integral of `pillarRadiance` is rewritten for the GPU without changing what it computes: the sines at each point come from the sine and cosine of the tilt, the Gaussians share one exponential with the dimming and one without it, the dust density's exponential also gives the dust column at the crystal, the slab reflectance takes one division, and the constants of the pixel and of the light are taken once. The light's elevation and distance come from the vertex stage as flat values. Within `0.01` blocks of rise, the mean density takes a linear correction of the density at the crystal instead of an exponential at the middle.
- `/calculatedSky pillars fill` switches a fill view (`LightPillarRenderer.toggleFillView`, uniform `fillView`): every pixel a ribbon covers adds green where its pillar shows at least half a display step and red where the fragment stage runs for nothing.
- `docs/climate/calculated-sky.md` describes the glint value, the rewritten integral, the fill view and its command, and the GPU time measured before the change.

## Decisions

- empireu measured `1.34 ms` of GPU time for `44` slots of a small torch field at about `1080` pixels with glitter at `75` micrometers, `1.14 ms` in a second reading, `0.34 ms` with smooth pillars, and `0.03 ms` facing away, on `2026-11-13`. The plan's budget for all new sky effects together is `0.5 ms`. The vertex stage is not worth changing.
- A scratch probe of the ribbons put the cost of glitter in the ribbon area, not in the draws per pixel: holding every pixel where a lone glint could reach half a step at `0.2%` made the ribbons of torches `8` to `32` blocks away `2.45` times the area of smooth ones, and the wobbling plates of a torch `32` blocks away `4.8` times. With the glint value at `1`, `2`, `4`, and `8` display steps they are `2.45`, `1.81`, `1.47`, and `1.21` times, and all the probe's lights `1.69`, `1.34`, `1.20`, and `1.11` times. empireu first chose four steps: a single pixel that much brighter shows against the night sky, fainter lone glints do not.
- After the change, empireu read `0.45 ms` at `75` micrometers and `0.226 ms` with smooth pillars, so glitter still cost about half. The fill view showed ribbons of near pillars and the upper parts red with green specks. At `75` micrometers the probe gave torch ribbons `1.63` times the smooth area at four steps and `0.2%`, `1.47` at `1%`, `1.32` at eight steps and `0.2%`, and `1.11` at eight steps and `1%`. empireu chose eight steps and `1%` on `2026-11-13`. The cost is at the outer edges of glittering pillars only: lone glints between four and eight steps, and a glint in about `1%` of cells per period just outside a ribbon. `lightPillarRibbonsHoldTheGlints` now bounds the chance at `1.5` times `RIBBON_GLINT_CHANCE` away from the ends and `10` times it anywhere, where the probe gave `0.87%` and `4.8%`.
- empireu asked for the fill view to see how many pixels are computed for nothing, and to keep it in the final code behind the command.
- The first GameTest run failed `lightPillarRibbonsHoldTheGlints`: pixels just outside the middle rows showed the glint value with a chance of `2.3%`. In each such case one crystal showed `1.00` to `1.03` times the glint value, where the count it takes jumps between `1` and `2`, and the half rows whose widths the ribbon takes fell just below it while the pixel between them was just above. A scratch probe of the test's cases gave a worst chance of `0.16%` with a margin of `5%` and `0.09%` to `0.16%` with `10%`, for `1.0%` and `1.6%` more ribbon area at `65` micrometers. `10%` leaves room for lights the test does not cover. The old rule had the same jump at the visible value, but it rarely fell in the middle rows of the test's lights.
- The near-crystal linear correction replaces an exponential that, written as a choice, the GPU would likely evaluate at every point. Over less than `0.005` blocks at a scale height of `80` blocks it differs from the exponential by about `2e-9`.

## Validation

- Computed outside the game on `2026-11-13` with `ShaderPortProbe`, a line by line port of the rewritten shader math to Java in the contributor's scratch workspace: over `200000` random lights, elevations, lateral angles, dust bottoms, extinctions, scenes, near fades, and horizons, it matches `dustRadiance` within `4.5e-10`, `dustCrystalCount` within `4.7e-10`, and `widestLateralSpread` within `2e-13`, relative. Ported to single precision, the rewritten and the old shader math have the same errors against the double reference for the radiance (median `5.9e-7` and `5.8e-7`, `99%` at `2.3e-4` for both), and for the count the rewrite's worst case is smaller (`69` against `3e5` relative, at counts far below a crystal).
- Both shader stages pass `glslangValidator`.
- A read-only review by a second model on `2026-11-13` found the rewritten math, the flat values, the three ribbon rules, and the fill view consistent. It found that a `pillarNearFade` below about `4e-39`, a denormal float, made the near weight infinity minus infinity, so NaN, where the old `smoothstep` gave `1`. The offset is now the constant `-1/3`, which removes the overflow. It also noted that `LightPillarOptics` keeps the density at the middle of the path within `SMALLEST_RISE`, which the shader replaces as described above, so the two agree to about `1e-9` there.
- GameTests on `2026-11-13`, `runGameTestServer -PgameTestNamespaces=frostedheart_calculated_sky` with JDK 17: the first run failed `lightPillarRibbonsHoldTheGlints` as above, the second, with the margin, passed all `51` tests, and the third, with eight steps and `1%`, passes all `51`, with the chance at most `0.87%` away from the ends of a ribbon, `4.1%` anywhere, and the mean on the edges at most `0.964` times the visible value. The figures below are from the second run. `lightPillarRibbonsHoldTheGlints` now checks the glint value, adds `65` micrometers, and checks that the edges of glittering ribbons show at most `1.01` times the visible value on average. Measured: a pixel on the edge shows the glint value with a chance of at most `0.16%` away from the ends of the ribbon and `3.8%` at the top rows, and at most `0.955` times the visible value on average.
- In game on `2026-11-13`, empireu read `0.45 ms` of GPU time after the change with `pillarCrystalSize` at `75` micrometers, the setting of the readings before, and `0.226 ms` with smooth pillars, against `0.336 ms` before. A first reading of `0.375 ms` was at `0.75` micrometers by a typo, where about `5e7` times as many crystals line up per pixel and the pillars look smooth.
- With eight steps and `1%`, empireu read `0.37 ms` at `75` micrometers in the same spot on `2026-11-13` and saw no visible change.
- empireu looked at the fill view on `2026-11-13` (screenshots `java_Ft6Y2ALUxe.png` and `java_670l7ppmK4.png`). The torch field's ribbons stack to yellow, the ribbons of near pillars and the upper parts are red with green specks where cells glint, broad red sheets of wobbling plates reach the top of the screen, and ribbons over trees are red where the scene hides the crystals. At `75` micrometers, ribbons of torches `8` to `32` blocks away cover, by the scratch probe, `1.63` times the area of smooth ones, against `2.56` before the change.
