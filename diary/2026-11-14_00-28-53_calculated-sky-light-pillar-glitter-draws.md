## Completed

Light pillars draw their glitter counts and sum their integral with less work, after `2026-11-13_22-31-49_calculated-sky-light-pillar-gpu-time.md` (`d51fbfba0`).

- Above a mean count of `3` (`LARGEST_COUNTED_MEAN`, `16` before), `crystalsDrawn` in `light_pillars.glsl` and `LightPillarOptics` takes a normal deviate by the Box Muller transform, as the path above `16` did, and rounds the Cornish Fisher expansion of the Poisson quantile to a whole count instead of using the deviate as a normal count. Up to `3`, the shader's new `crystalsCounted` sums the Poisson distribution once for the counts of this period and the next, and returns the same counts as two separate sums. `LightPillarOptics.crystalsDrawn` is package-private for the new GameTest.
- In `tiltSum`, the eye's leg times its mean density is the dust column at the crystal less the eye's, times `1 / sin e` taken once per pixel, which removes a division per point. The count is summed only while `crystalCountScale` is above `0`, and the widest spread only in the vertex stage.
- `docs/climate/calculated-sky.md` describes the draws and the two changes to the integral.
- The shader takes the integral with the `4` point Gauss Legendre rule over tilts within `3 sigma` instead of `8` points within `4 sigma`. `LightPillarOptics` keeps `8` points within `4 sigma` as the reference. The docs give the errors.
- `/calculatedSky pillars debug <mode>` (`LightPillarRenderer.setDebugMode`, uniform `debugMode`) leaves out one part of the fragment stage to measure its cost: `1` the integral, replaced by a flat shade where the scene lies beyond the light, and with it the glitter; `2` the glitter's draws and count sum, with the ribbons as wide; `3` the scene depth, neither bound nor read; `4` everything but flat ribbons. The vertex stage is the same in every mode. The docs describe it.

## Decisions

- On `2026-11-13`, after the last entry, empireu asked for an approximation of the integral and allowed up to `10` read-only Subordinate sessions for ideas. Six sessions (DeepSeek, about `4` minutes each) looked at the integral analytically, at quadrature rules, at the cost per point, at fragments computed for nothing, at the glitter, and at the wobbling plates. Of their ideas, empireu chose on `2026-11-14` the glitter draws and the exact savings in the integral. The approximations of the integral (a Gauss rule for the tilt Gaussian cut to the range matched the 8 point rule away from the lights with `2.9` instead of `5.4` evaluations per pixel in a scratch probe) and the area of the wobbling plates' ribbons stay open.
- Two sessions proposed the gamma form of the Wilson Hilferty approximation for the draws. A scratch probe compared the shares `min(k / N, cap)` from closed forms of the quantile with the Poisson distribution's at the same chances. The gamma form keeps the mean but has twice the Poisson distribution's skew and never draws `0`: its Kolmogorov Smirnov distance is `0.108` at `N = 6`. The plain normal distribution of the old path is `0.066` at `N = 16`, where it began. The rounded Cornish Fisher expansion is `0.002` at `N = 6` and `0.005` at `N = 2`, and keeps whole counts. Its average is `0.4%` above the mean value at `N = 2` and `1.3%` at `1.25`, so the sum stays up to `3`.
- Sharing one Box Muller pair between the counts of this period and the next, as one session proposed, would draw the next period's first count differently from this period's second, so cells would jump at the period's end. Each count keeps its own deviate.
- The sessions also proposed writing the light's leg times its mean density as the dust column it rises through over `sin(e - 2 tau)`, and the across scale as `cos(beta - tau) / (cos tau sin(e - beta))`. Both identities hold. In single precision the first lost accuracy where the light's leg is long and nearly level, since that sine is a difference of nearly equal products, and the second saved nothing, so neither was kept.

- After the debug mode showed the integral takes most of the time, empireu set the standard for approximations on `2026-11-14`: they only need to look about right, not to match within a display step. A review by another agent proposed a Gauss Hermite rule where nothing cuts the tilt window, then a Gaussian rule built from the moments of the window. The first rarely applies to wobbling plates (tilt spread `5 degrees`), whose window the crystals at the eye cut wherever the elevation plus the light's elevation is below `40 degrees`. The second needs an erf approximation, moments up to the seventh in single precision, and two fallbacks to the old rule for narrow windows and windows in the tail. A relative error of about `1.7%` makes one display step at the most sensitive brightness, so the review's step counts were about `34` times too high. Gauss Legendre with fewer points over a range cut to `3 sigma` does as well with no fallback, and of the probe's choices empireu took fewer points and the cut range. Per-pillar texels at a stable lower resolution, which would make the point count matter little, were left for later.

## Validation

- Computed outside the game on `2026-11-14` with `ShaderPortProbe` updated to the shader: over `200000` random cases the double port matches `dustRadiance` within `4.5e-10`, `dustCrystalCount` within `4.7e-10`, and `widestLateralSpread` within `1.8e-13`, relative, and in single precision the errors against the double reference are those of the shader before (median `5.9e-7`, `99%` at `2.3e-4`).
- A copy of the new GameTest `lightPillarCrystalDrawsFollowThePoissonDistribution`, run outside the game against the changed `LightPillarOptics` compiled on its own, gives Kolmogorov Smirnov distances of at most `0.0036` from the Poisson distribution for means from `0.3` to `10000` over `200000` draws each, all whole counts, and `lightPillarSparkleKeepsTheMeanValue`'s averages are within `0.04%` of the mean value from `N = 4` up.
- Both shader stages pass `glslangValidator`, with the glitter draws, with the debug mode, and with the `4` point rule. `compileJava` passes with the debug mode.
- GameTests on `2026-11-14`, `runGameTestServer -PgameTestNamespaces=frostedheart_calculated_sky` with JDK 17: all `52` pass, the new `lightPillarCrystalDrawsFollowThePoissonDistribution` included.
- In game on `2026-11-14`, empireu read `0.8 ms` of GPU time at `75` micrometers and `0.66 ms` with smooth pillars, against `0.37 ms` and `0.226 ms` in the last entry. Both are `0.43 ms` higher. With smooth pillars the fragment stage only lost work (no glitter draws, one division less per point, no count sum), so the rise does not come from the change. In a second session in the same world and spot, with the same setup, empireu read `0.06 ms` facing away, `0.9 ms` at `75` micrometers, and `0.67 ms` with smooth pillars with the new shader, and the same numbers after a restart with the committed shader in `src` (the only built copy, `build/resources/main`, was then the committed file). So the two changes save nothing measurable there, and the split of the time estimated in the last entry (about `0.20 ms` for the integral, `0.14 ms` for the glitter loops) does not hold. Why the same spot reads about `0.43 ms` more than the day before is not known.
- In game on `2026-11-14`, in a third session at the same spot at `75` micrometers, empireu read the GPU time in each debug mode: `0.808 ms` in mode `0`, `0.150 ms` in `1`, `0.730 ms` in `2`, `0.778 ms` in `3`, and `0.133 ms` in `4`. So the integral takes about `0.58 ms` (mode `2` less mode `1`), `72%` of the draw, the glitter's draws and count sum about `0.078 ms` (mode `0` less mode `2`), the scene depth read with the rest of the per-pixel setup at most about `0.02 ms` (mode `1` less mode `4`, and mode `0` less mode `3` is `0.03 ms` with the pixels behind the scene computing in mode `3`), and the vertex stage with the flat ribbons `0.133 ms`. The parts add up to the total.
- In game on `2026-11-14`, the `4` point rule within `3 sigma` and the `8` point rule within `4 sigma`, swapped by copying each into `build/resources/main` and reloading with F3+T, read `0.85 ms` and `0.836 ms`, and then `0.6 ms`, in the same session. Neither a view of the torch field from a hill, a close view of the field, nor a close view near a torch showed a difference that empireu could tell. The screenshot harness could not take the comparison, since pillars fade in after each move and it does not wait for them. In fullscreen with the NVIDIA driver's power management set to prefer maximum performance, the `4` point rule read a steady `0.25 ms`. The laptop GPU lowers its clock while it is not fully loaded, so the earlier readings, the debug mode split above included, measured the clock as much as the work.
- F3+T did not load a shader copied into `build/resources/main`: a red-tinted copy left the pillars gold. So the swaps above compared a shader with itself, the blind screenshots included, and only restarts with the shader in `src` count.
- After a restart, with the `4` point rule and the same settings, empireu read `0.25 ms` in debug mode `0`, `0.06 ms` in `1`, `0.212 ms` in `2`, `0.24 ms` in `3`, and `0.051 ms` in `4`. So the integral takes `0.152 ms` (`61%`), the glitter `0.038 ms`, the scene depth and the per-pixel setup about `0.01 ms`, and the vertex stage with the flat ribbons `0.051 ms`.
- After another restart with the `8` point rule within `4 sigma` in `src`, tinted red to show it loaded, empireu read `0.372 ms`, `0.061 ms`, `0.318 ms`, `0.367 ms`, and `0.051 ms` in modes `0` to `4`. So the `4` point rule takes the integral from `0.257 ms` to `0.152 ms` and the glitter from `0.054 ms` to `0.038 ms`, as the count sum also runs per point, and the draw from `0.372 ms` to `0.25 ms`. With the untinted `8` point rule loaded by another restart, empireu could not tell the same views apart from the `4` point rule's.
